## Level 2 (2D planar Navier-Stokes, slot-jet arrays)
| ID | Assumption | Value | Status | Basis | Effect if wrong |

| L2 | Slot-width Reynolds number depends only on slot count | Re = ρQ / (μ N L) | derived | Continuity | — |

| L5 | Jet inlet profile | Parabolic (developed slot flow), temperature uniform at inlet value | assumed | Nozzle plate thicker than a few slot widths | Flat profile would lower stagnation h slightly |
| L6 | Pressure drop | Inlet-slot face to return-slot face; nozzle-plate and manifold losses excluded | assumed | Same core-only convention as Level 1 | System ΔP is higher |
| L7 | Return path | One return slot midway between jets, same width as the jet | chosen | Makes the unit cell periodic | Other return layouts change the wall-jet length |
| L8 | Minimum jet gap and slot width | 0.1 mm | chosen | Riccardo: 50 µm laser-cut features exist but only at low volume. ## Level 2 (plan view: fin planforms in the tall-fin limit)
| ID | Assumption | Value | Status | Basis | Effect if wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | Tall-fin limit | 2D solve in the plane of the fin layer; floor and cover drag and heat transfer are not in the 2D solve; the floor gets the side-wall h | assumed, measured | Gate G3: for the Level 1 recommended straight design (0.387 / 0.387 / 3.75 mm, `validation/level2_planview_straight.json`) the plan view gives ΔP 7.1% and R_th 5.6% below the Level 1 CFD, which includes floor and cover | Plan-view ΔP and R_th read low by roughly that amount for every topology; larger for short fins (H/gap small), where the floor and cover matter more |
| V2 | Lateral symmetry, steady laminar flow | Strip topologies (offset strips, pins) solved on half a lateral pitch with symmetry planes; channels solved as one channel; steady Newton solution | assumed | Re_Dh 71–216 (interstitial velocity, plan Dh) at rated flow; pins Re_d,max 50–136 (gap velocity, pin diameter or diamond side); a steady symmetric solution exists and Newton converged for all 63 designs, but no stability analysis is done | Staggered pins and strips may shed vortices at these Re (an isolated cylinder sheds above Re_d ≈ 47; arrays delay it); shedding would raise h and ΔP; 3D Dean vortices in wavy channels are absent in 2D, so wavy-fin benefit is understated |
| V3 | Wall heat flux | Uniform flux on all fin walls in the 2D solve; one mean h per design = q'' / (period-mean wall T − period-mean velocity-weighted bulk T) | assumed | Standard developed-periodic definition (Patankar, Liu & Sparrow 1977); reduces to Nu = 8.235 for straight fins | Real fins have non-uniform flux (more near the base); local h variation around pins is lumped into one value |
| V4 | Floor heat transfer | Floor (plan fluid area) gets the same h as the fin sides, η = 1 | assumed | Same structure as Level 1 | The floor is a different surface (boundary layer of the end wall); effect is part of V1 |
| V5 | Fin efficiency | Adiabatic tip, η = tanh(mH)/(mH), m = sqrt(h P_c / (k_s A_c)) with the plan-section perimeter and area of one fin | assumed | Standard for pins and strips; for a continuous wall it is the Level 1 formula | Ignores spreading in the base under sparse pins and 2D conduction at strip ends; pins at H = 4 mm have η 0.4–0.6, so R_th is sensitive to it |
| V6 | Developed-periodic values over the whole length | No entrance effect; checked with a developing strip from a uniform inlet for one design per topology | assumed, measured | Pins and offset strips reach the periodic h within 2% in 3–5 periods (4–10 mm); wavy channels need 10 periods (20 mm); straight channels from the 20–25 mm window | Real h is higher near the inlet, so R_th is conservative; the length-mean h of a straight channel over 60 mm is 6.9% above the developed value (`validation/level2_planview_straight.py`); comparable for wavy channels, smaller for pins and strips |
| V7 | Manufacturing limits | Solid feature ≥ 0.15 mm, fluid gap ≥ 0.15 mm (true minimum distance between solids), H ≤ 4 mm, H / minimum fin thickness ≤ 10; Level 1's H / gap ≤ 10 is now applied too (SPEC Amendment 3), with gap = the true minimum fluid gap: designs that break it are solved but excluded from fronts and the recommendation and listed with the reason | chosen | Riccardo, SPEC Amendments 2 and 3 | Excludes 36 of 78 converged designs, including every design with gaps under 0.4 mm at H = 4 mm |
| V8 | Planform construction | Wavy walls offset ± w/2 along the normal (constant normal gap); lateral pitch set so the thinnest fin section is t; zigzag corners rounded to a 0.05 mm inner wall radius; offset strips with g = 0 and rows not overlapping laterally; pin cells cut through row-A pin centres | chosen | Simplest constructions that meet the brief; sharp-corner zigzags would put a singularity in the offset wall | Fins are thicker than t at wavy crests (more solid, fewer channels per width) |
| V9 | Lateral layout | Continuum: flow per lateral unit = Q × (unit width) / 60 mm; no integer channel count, no edge channels | assumed | Same footprint and total flow for every topology | For straight fins the Level 1 integer count (69 channels in 59.75 mm) differs by 0.4% |

Note on R9 (2026-11-15): the cut-off now has a sensitivity study (0.05 to 0.60, `examples/output/gpu_coldplate/report.md` and
`examples/output/level2_planview/summary.md`). Level 1: the pick (0.39 / 0.39 / 3.75 mm, 14.05 mK/W at 1.60 kPa) holds from 0.20 to
0.25; at 0.30 and above the pick is 0.42 / 0.39 / 3.75 mm (14.90 mK/W at 1.29 kPa), from 0.35 up through the objective clause; at
0.15 and below it moves to higher ΔP (12.79 mK/W at 2.64 kPa, then 11.92 at 4.66). Across plan-view topologies the pick (circular
pins d 0.4, 0.8 × 0.9 mm, 12.65 mK/W at 1.42 kPa) holds only at 0.25: 0.20 picks a denser pin array (11.88 at 1.86 kPa) and 0.30 a
sparser one (13.78 at 1.03 kPa). Every pick from 0.05 to 0.60 meets the objective at both levels. The cut-off is a judgment that sets
where on the front the pick lands; it does not decide which topology wins (circular pins at every cut-off).

Note on V1 and V6 (2026-11-15): `validation/level2_planview_straight.py` takes the Level 1 recommended design from
`examples/output/gpu_coldplate/results.json`, so after Amendment 4 gate G3 runs on 0.387 / 0.387 / 3.75 mm instead of
0.422 / 0.444 / 4.0 mm. The plan view reads ΔP 7.06% and R_th 5.63% below the Level 1 CFD (was 7.1% and 5.7%), and the length-mean
h of the developing straight channel over 60 mm is 6.9% above the developed value (was about 8%). The rows above keep the values
measured on the earlier design; V1 and V6 now carry the current values (updated 2026-11-18).

| P9 | Hydrodynamically developing flow from a uniform inlet | ΔP = ΔP_fd + K(∞)·ρU²/2, U the channel mean velocity, K(∞) = 0.680 (parallel plates), used for every aspect ratio. Replaces (2026-11-15) the Muzychka & Yovanovich Part I apparent-friction blend, whose long-channel tail was worth about 0.98–0.99·ρU²/2 | sourced | Ray, Durst & Ray (2017), arXiv:1707.04947, Table 2: parallel-plate channel, Kn = 10⁻⁴, C2 = 0, K1 = 0.6802, the high-Re asymptote of K_fd from Navier–Stokes solves with a uniform inlet (full text read; docs/literature.md, fourth pass). Their K0/Re term (K0 = 80.3, Re on Dh = 2 × gap) is not used: it comes from axial diffusion upstream of a profile imposed at the inlet plane, which a plenum-fed plate does not have | Resolved tall-fin solves with a slip lead-in give 0.69–0.70 at Re (2w) 125–218, so in the plate limit the term is within 3%. For the real duct (aspect ratio about 0.1) no open K(∞) was found; corner development may raise it. At 0.68·ρU²/2 the term is 17 Pa (1.1% of core ΔP) for the recommended design |
| P10 | Thermally and simultaneously developing heat transfer, uniform wall flux | Local Nu_√A from Eq. 50 (Graetz) and Eqs. 51–52 (combined entry) with Table 2 constants C3 = 0.501, f(Pr) Eq. 48, m = 2.27 + 1.65 Pr^(1/3), local form; the entrance asymptotes are blended with the screen's own Shah & London Nu_H1 (converted to √A) instead of the source's Eq. 31 (re-anchored 2026-11-15), so the long-channel limit is exactly the developed model | sourced | Muzychka & Yovanovich (2002), Part II (full text); ±12% (Graetz), ±15% (combined entry), 0.1 < Pr < ∞; tests reproduce their z*_t worked values and asymptotes, and that the anchored blend reaches the given developed value | The source's Eq. 31 reads 19% above the exact 8.235 for parallel plates, which hid part of the entrance gain (first gate failure, −36%). | P11 | Effective h over the length | Harmonic length-mean of the local h (R_th uses the mean of wall − bulk temperature under uniform flux) | assumed | Definition of R_th with a uniform flux per unit length; the resolved check uses the same mean | Axial conduction in the copper base shifts heat toward the inlet; the true effective h lies between the harmonic and the arithmetic mean, so the R_th reduction is understated |
| P12 | Inlet and outlet loss coefficients | Kays (1950) laminar Kc, Ke for flat and square ducts, polynomial in σ, linear in aspect ratio (Kc 0.71–0.75, Ke −0.25 to +0.06 over the 18 designs); σ = w/(w + t), plenum as tall as the fins; ΔP_io = (Kc + Ke)ρU²/2, reported separately and not added to the core ΔP or to any combined figure | sourced (secondary) | dos Reis & Altemani (COBEM 1999), Eq. 5 and Table 1 (full text); Kays's original not obtained; flat-duct Ke fit checked against the momentum balance with a parabolic profile | Resolved plenum-to-plenum solves (tall-fin limit) give inlet + outlet −0.10 to +0.22·ρU²/2 against Kc + Ke of 0.43–0.80, a nearly constant gap (0.53–0.57) close to the channel's own entrance excess, so the laminar Kc appears to contain part of the entrance pressure defect and adding it to K(∞) would count it twice (not confirmed from the source). 20 Pa (1.3%) for the recommended design; outside the channel-core ΔP definition |
